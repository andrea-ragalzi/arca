# S2-11: Shared station finish and lowered ceilings

## Brief

Make the four connected spaces read as adjoining departments of one research station. Fix the maintenance room first: today it differs in finish and wall construction. Use one wall family (full bay, opening, corner and corner joint) and the same `.tres` resources everywhere: steel skirting 0.00–0.10 m, grey-green washable band 0.10–1.10 m, clinical white above, industrial blue frames, a continuous dark resin floor and a 0.60 m panel ceiling with repeated industrial luminaires. Lower the ceilings in geometry to 2.65 m in the rooms and 2.50 m in the corridor, keep fittings over the main path at 2.30 m or higher, apply the given sRGB palette, and verify the route with the playable character and the TPS camera. Preserve doors, triggers, interactions, navigation and predator routes. Justify any height that the camera or a passage forces away from the target.

### Departures from the proposed heights

- **Route openings: 3.0 m wide by 2.40 m high, instead of the 1.10 × 2.10 m standard door.** Every route opening is also a predator route. The navigation mesh uses a 0.6 m agent radius. The existing sliding leaf and locked gate are 3.2 m wide. A 2.40 m head keeps the default spring arm (1.6 m pivot, 2.4 m arm, 20° down) from contracting each time Regina crosses a threshold. A 2.10 m head would cut that line from about 1.3 m past the wall.
- **Two raised 3.10 m bays.** One spans the corridor at z 16.5–22.5 over the marked crate leap. The other spans maintenance at x −6.6 to −0.4, z −14 to −9.6, over the diagnostic table. The raptor's leap arc reaches a capsule top of about 2.99 m onto the crate and 2.90 m onto the table. Even standing on the crate, it reaches 2.56 m, above the 2.50 m corridor. Rebuilding without the bays failed six physical-combat checks for the crate jump, the table jump and the descent. White soffit steps with a steel edge and collision close each change of height.
- **Live-cargo hatch head: 2.25 m.** The 0.40 m header carries the hatch sign and a safety edge. The raptor emerges with room to spare.

### Performance

The setup was Godot 4.5.1, X11, OpenGL 3.3 Compatibility, an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3050 Laptop GPU with driver 580.159.03, 1280 × 720, VSync off and Dummy audio. The renderer, resolution and views were the same for both columns. The baseline is the S2-10 working tree rebuilt in a temporary copy. Each architecture view warms for 60 frames and samples for 2 s.

| Area | Lowest mean FPS, before → after | Worst P95 | Worst P99 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Maintenance | 490.2 → 392.4 | 2.84 → 4.20 ms | 3.37 → 4.84 ms |
| Security | 445.6 → 364.6 | 3.63 → 4.50 ms | 5.11 → 5.49 ms |
| Control office | 769.6 → 665.0 | 2.04 → 2.64 ms | 3.09 → 3.36 ms |
| Lift and freight | 1198.5 → 846.4 | 1.37 → 1.99 ms | 1.73 → 2.58 ms |

In the TPS walk, the same tool and route ran in both trees with movement held: mean 489.5 → 460.8 FPS, P95 5.02 → 4.99 ms and P99 6.76 → 6.76 ms. Draw calls fell, for example from 2,216 to 1,030 in the heaviest maintenance view, but static views cost up to about 1.4 ms more at P95. The likely cause is fragment work from the world triplanar finishes now covering every wall, floor and ceiling pixel. Repeated runs vary by about 10 %. Every figure stays far inside the 16.67 ms budget at 60 FPS. No extra shadowed lights were added; each area keeps at most one.

### Remaining limits

- **Aiming hard against a wall.** The upstream aim pose moves the spring-arm origin 0.6 m to the side. Hugging a wall within about 0.7 m while aiming can place that origin at the wall surface, and the sphere sweep cannot correct an origin that is already inside. This predates the change and is not caused by the ceilings.
- **Grazing-angle moiré.** The ceiling grid shows some moiré at grazing angles through the double frame at the airlock.
